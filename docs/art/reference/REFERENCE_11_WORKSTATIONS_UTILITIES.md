# Reference 11 — Workstations & Utilities

## Purpose

Define the visual grammar for functional camp work areas and utility structures.

Approved visual companion:

- `visual/REFERENCE_11_WORKSTATIONS_UTILITIES.png`

---

# Core principle

A workstation should communicate its purpose through **surface, support, attachments and nearby organization**, not through decorative complexity.

The family should look improvised, sturdy and readable from the gameplay camera.

---

# Core workstation families

Use a compact vocabulary:

- work table;
- workbench;
- chopping stump;
- food-prep surface;
- flat-rock work surface;
- drying rack;
- fire/cooking supports;
- wash station;
- rain-catch setup;
- water-vessel stand;
- drainage setup;
- tool rack;
- hanging hook frame;
- shelf;
- peg post;
- material staging area.

---

# Evolution language

Prefer progression such as:

```text
natural surface
→ crude table
→ reinforced work surface
→ organized workbench
→ covered utility area
```

The visible increase should be in capability and organization:

- more stable surface;
- more usable area;
- attached storage;
- weather protection;
- better tool/material access.

Do not signal progression primarily through ornament.

---

# Construction grammar

Most utility objects should reuse:

- posts;
- beams;
- planks;
- braces;
- rope bindings;
- hooks;
- shelves;
- simple cloth/thatch covers.

This allows visual coherence and procedural reuse.

---

# Project-support pieces

Temporary construction support should also follow the same grammar:

- scaffold;
- brace frame;
- temporary support frame;
- staged materials;
- simple hoist/hanging support where needed.

These pieces should look intentionally temporary, not like broken final assets.

---

# Damage and repair

Useful visible states include:

```text
intact
loose
wet
partially broken
repaired
reinforced
```

Repairs should remain legible using broad pieces such as:

- replacement plank;
- extra brace;
- rebound lashing;
- patched cover;
- raised/dried replacement surface.

---

# Function-first silhouettes

Examples:

- drying rack: horizontal hanging line/frame with separated hanging items;
- workbench: broad stable surface with thick supports;
- wash station: basin/surface + water vessel relationship;
- rain catch: broad cloth/leaf catch plane feeding a container;
- tool rack: vertical/horizontal support with sparse, clearly separated tools.

Avoid silhouettes that become generic furniture.

---

# Material policy

Default:

- shared flat-color materials;
- low material count;
- visible bindings instead of texture detail;
- no wood grain textures;
- no ornate surface patterns;
- shared wetness/weathering when needed.

---

# Readability rule

A workstation should remain understandable even when the small loose props are removed.

Loose tools, food or containers may reinforce function, but the structure itself should communicate the primary utility.

---

# Acceptance criteria

A workstation family passes when:

- its function is apparent from gameplay distance;
- structure is chunky and stable;
- parts reuse the approved construction language;
- progression increases capability rather than ornament;
- nearby clutter is controlled;
- damage/repair states remain readable;
- the object does not rely on texture or tiny accessories to explain itself.
